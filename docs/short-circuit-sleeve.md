# Short Circuit: the sleeve

The sleeve for "Tenement Lights (Short Circuit)" is drawn as an object that did not exist in 1983: a
microSD memory card, enlarged to the size of a 12-inch record sleeve. The idea is borrowed from the way the
sleeve for the 1983 single "Blue Monday" was made to look like a floppy disk, a piece of the technology of its
own day. A microSD card is about 22 years too early for that: the format appeared in 2005
([microSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SD_card#Micro_(microSD)) on Wikipedia).

The design is `docs/short-circuit-sleeve.html`, a standalone page with the front and back at 840 by 840 pixels (12 inches at 70 pixels to the inch). A rendered copy is `docs/short-circuit-sleeve.png`. Neither is part of the repository build.
It is an invented release by an invented artist and label, so it carries no real band's or label's name or mark.

- **Shape.** A square of black card with the top right corner cut away, as on a memory card. On the back the cut
  is at the top left, so the card is the same card seen from behind.
- **Die cut.** A round window shows the record, with a gold label that reads "128 GB" and "3:00". Where a floppy has
  its head window, this has the grooves.
- **Contacts.** Eight gold strips along the bottom edge, which are the pins of the card and the only colour on
  the front besides the code.
- **Write-protect notch.** A small notch cut in the left edge.
- **Type.** Lower case, in a geometric sans serif (Jost, a free face in the Futura tradition), and a specification
  in small print in place of a title: "microSDXC", "UHS-I", "A2", "V30", "100 MB/s".
- **Colour code.** Four squares down the right edge, red, amber, teal and blue, spell the catalogue number TL83,
  with the key on the back. This borrows the idea that the sleeve names the record in a code rather than in words.
- **Back.** The two sides with their times, a specification table (capacity 128 GB, contents 9 minutes 53 seconds),
  a credit line for the machines, and the key to the code.

The joke is the mismatch: a card that holds about 128 billion bytes carrying less than ten minutes of music, on a
record that holds 15, in a year when a hard disk held ten million bytes.
